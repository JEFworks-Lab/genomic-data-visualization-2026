### 1. How do the gene loadings on the first PC relate to features of the genes such as its mean or variance
  #### Panel A (Mean vs PC1 loading) [homework/hw2/pmuseng1_1.png]
  - There appears to be a positive linear trend
  - Genes with higher mean expression tend to have larger positive PC1 loadings
  - But there is clustering of low-mean genes near zero

  #### Panel B (Variance vs PC1 loading) [homework/hw2/pmuseng1_2.png]
  - The trend appears stronger
  - Genes with higher variance tend to have larger positive PC1 loadings
  - The spread is wider along variance than mean

  #### Interpretation
  - PC1 appears to be more strongly driven by gene variance than by mean expression
  - This is biologically expected since PCA maximizes variance, so high-variance genes disproportionately influence PC1

### 2. Write a description explaining what you are trying to make salient and why you believe your data visualization  
  - This data visualization explores how gene loadings on the first principal component (PC1) relate to gene-level quantitative features, specifically mean expression and variance
  - The data types visualized are quantitative: PC1 loading, mean expression, and variance
  - These are encoded using the geometric primitive of points, with x-position and y-position as visual channels
  - According to the perceptual ranking discussed in class, position is the most effective encoding for quantitative data, making this choice appropriate for assessing continuous relationships
  - The two-panel design enhances salience by separating the relationships into distinct visual spaces. By placing mean expression in Panel A and variance in Panel B, the viewer can easily compare how each feature relates to PC1 loadings without visual clutter
  - The visualization effectively enhances salience by using position, avoiding redundant encodings, minimizing cognitive load and allowing trends to be perceived through Gestalt principles of continuity (via the regression line)

```r
df <- read.csv(
  "/Users/pmusenge/Desktop/genomic_data_vis/genomic-data-visualization-2026/data/Xenium-IRI-ShamR_matrix.csv.gz"
)

# Inspecting structure
dim(df)
colnames(df)[1:10]


# Extracting the gene expression matrix
gene_matrix <- as.matrix(df[, 4:ncol(df)])

# check and ensure numeric
gene_matrix <- apply(gene_matrix, 2, as.numeric)
dim(gene_matrix)
head(gene_matrix)

# log transformation
gene_matrix_log <- log1p(gene_matrix)
dim(gene_matrix_log)

pca <- prcomp(gene_matrix_log, scale. = TRUE)

# Extracting PC1 gene loadings
pc1_loadings <- pca$rotation[, 1]
head(pc1_loadings)

gene_mean <- colMeans(gene_matrix_log)
head(gene_mean)
gene_var  <- apply(gene_matrix_log, 2, var)
head(gene_var)

gene_df <- data.frame(
  gene = names(pc1_loadings),
  PC1_loading = pc1_loadings,
  mean_expression = gene_mean,
  variance = gene_var
)

library(ggplot2)

# Panel A
p1 <- ggplot(gene_df, aes(x = mean_expression, y = PC1_loading)) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.6) +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm", se = FALSE, color = "red") +
  labs(
    title = "Panel A: PC1 Loadings vs Gene Mean",
    x = "Mean log1p Expression",
    y = "PC1 Loading"
  ) +
  theme_minimal()
p1
# Panel B
p2 <- ggplot(gene_df, aes(x = variance, y = PC1_loading)) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.6) +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm", se = FALSE, color = "blue") +
  labs(
    title = "Panel B: PC1 Loadings vs Gene Variance",
    x = "Gene Variance (log1p)",
    y = "PC1 Loading"
  ) +
  theme_minimal()

p2
```
